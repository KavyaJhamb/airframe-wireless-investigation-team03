The Wi‑Fi didn’t break. The login server did.

Every client on the 802.1X network failed to log in, on every access point and every channel, while the IoT devices on the PSK network connected. Replaying the captures, Airframe sent the right alert to the right team 5 seconds after the first failed login.

From “Wi‑Fi is down” to the right team in seconds

Tesla already runs a fleet of passive sensors that capture 802.11 headers. Airframe turns those captures into ranked, owned incidents: what broke, where in the login sequence, how many devices, and which team should look first. No payloads, no transmitting, no new hardware.

Why it can be trusted

Judgment built in

A roam is not a failure. One client heard by several sensors is one finding. A capture that ends mid‑join is not a failure. Each rule has a test.

Honest about blind spots

When a sensor hears only the AP, the finding says so. “Observed”, “consistent with” and “not proven” are kept apart in every alert.

Private by design

MAC addresses and network names are replaced with salted hashes before analysis. Login identities are never read.

How it works

Header‑only captures in, ranked incidents out. The best of two independent implementations, cross‑checked against each other and against 32 test captures.

  1. Read and mask

    Parse 802.11 and 802.1X headers from every sensor. Replace MACs and network names with salted hashes first.

  2. Rebuild every join

    Merge sensors per client and replay auth, association, 802.1X and the key handshake. Record where each attempt stopped and why.

  3. Judge, then group

    Separate roams, client exits and capture edges from real failures. Roll failures up into one incident per shared cause.

  4. Route the alert

    Send each incident to its owner with the evidence, the first check, a confidence level and what is not proven.

Privacy checked

For every scenario, the tests search all outputs for the raw MACs and network names used to generate it. None may appear.

At scale

Sensors do the header extraction and send compact events, not captures. Each site correlates its own sensors by client, keeps summaries for everything and raw headers only around incidents. Alerts go to owners; a heartbeat from every sensor tells silent sensors apart from quiet air.
